    The physical backbone:
    connect the wards, stop the flood.

    Roads, drainage, water and transport are the sector that carries the heaviest gaps. The plan sequences drainage before road, pools ward funds with the municipal purse, and standardises right-of-way so the hills stop being cut off every monsoon.

    The situation · Ch5.1.1

    A connected core on paper, a cut-off one on the ground

    The bazaar core (Wards 1–4) runs on 5–8 m rights-of-way against the 10–30 m Nepal Urban Road Standard (2011). The same geography that makes Baglung a hill market town makes its infrastructure expensive and fragile.

    The problem · v3 Fig 5.1

    Why the bazaar floods and the roads fail

    The problem tree runs four causes into one core — bazaar flooding and crash-prone roads — and four effects out the other side. It is a drainage failure first, a road failure second.

    The strategy · Ch5.1.4–5.1.6

    What we'll build · Tables 5.4–5.13

    Four programmes, one sequence

    The integrated strategy lands as four inter-locked programmes. Roads are itemised segment-by-segment from field surveys; water, drainage and transport complete the set.

    Road · 64.2 kmStrategic blacktop

    Ward 8–9 (2 km), 9–10 (5.6), 10–11 (5), 13–12 (5.6), 13–14 (6.6), inter-school links (1.6 + 0.5 + 1), Ward 6–Myagdi (6.9). Field-surveyed segments — the all-weather spine.

    Water4 × 700,000 L tanks

    Gravity-fed distribution to Wards 4, 6, 8 and 13. Demand 6.9 MLD vs 5 MLD capacity — the gap is noted and sized into the phasing.

    DrainageBazaar storm trunk

    Ward 2 has a 25 m drop to the Kali Gandaki yet still ponds — a continuous stormwater route (12 km trunk + 15 km catch) ends the annual flood.

    TransportBus park operationalised

    The Davgara park is under-used at 15–20 buses/day. Electric van home service and online ticketing lift ridership and repay the TDF debt.
