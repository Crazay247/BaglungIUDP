Fieldwork,
from the ground up.

Every number in this plan started with a ward visit. The team walked the bazaar, climbed the hill wards, ran focus groups and key-informant interviews, and came back with the problems, ideas and photos that seeded the policy direction.

How it was collected

The survey method

The fieldwork combined structured interviews, ward-level focus groups, key-informant interviews (KIIS) with municipal staff and sector authorities, direct observation and inventory photography.

KIIKey-informant interviews

Structured conversations with municipal officers, health-post staff, school principals and rescue units.

WardWard-level engagement

Meetings with ward officials across all 14 wards to capture local priorities and constraints.

ObservationDirect observation

Site visits to farms, livestock sheds, fish ponds, rescue stores and heritage structures.

SurveyHousehold & business surveys

Structured questionnaires feeding the six-sector IUDP dataset workbook.

HealthHealth-post inventory

Staffing and service audits at the 13 health posts against DUDBC norms.

DRRRescue & DRR inventory

Cataloguing rescue equipment and the disaster-preparedness gaps for the DRR sector.

From ideas to policy

The handwritten seed

The group's field notes — mostly Nepali shorthand on eight pages — were transcribed and turned directly into the sector proposals. They show the planning loop from the ground to the plan.

Environment (from the notes)

  • Solid-waste management — new landfill site, capacity building, treatment.
  • 3-bin source-segregation awareness.
  • Patch-identify disaster areas (flood/landslide) with bio-engineering.
  • Embankment control where the road cuts the slopes.
  • Flash-flood scenario and mitigation (“hanging carriers” where tractors can’t reach).

Physical (from the notes)

  • Include forest areas in the land-suitability map.
  • Identify 3–4 road and 1–2 traffic-safety projects.
  • Drinking-water: where to reach, identify distant boreholes.
  • Baglung–Myagdi road / suspension bridge proposal.
  • Public bus-station spot + a time schedule.
  • Drainage under-road pipelines.
  • Inclusive pedestrian sidewalks in the core market.

Social (from the notes)

  • Health staff-ratio analysis and incentives for hard-to-post hill stations.
  • Vector-borne, HIV/AIDs and skin-condition awareness.
  • Birthing centres for ward clusters (12–14, 9–11, 5–7).
  • Community & e-libraries, adult literacy.
  • Youth skill development (CAD, web, design, public speaking).
  • Weekly youth mental-health counselling — suicidal cases rising.
Traceability. These transcriptions are preserved verbatim (English/Nepali as written) in the source notes, then interpreted into the LFA problem→objective→alternative→risk trees behind every sector chapter.
