Questions · प्रश्नहरू

What to expect · के हुन्छ?

What actually happens in a first counselling session?
Mostly talking and listening. The counsellor asks how you've been feeling, what your days look like, and what you want to change. You set the pace, and you can decline any question. Nothing is forced, and you can stop the session at any time. You will not be judged or labelled.
Do I need to turn my camera on for a video session?
No. Audio-only and chat sessions are just as valid. Choose whatever feels safest for you when booking — video, phone, or chat.
Is what I say kept private?
Yes — professional counsellors follow strict confidentiality rules. The usual exception is if there is an immediate risk to your life or someone else's, in which case the counsellor will tell you honestly and bring in help. In this prototype, nothing you type is sent anywhere: bookings stay in your own browser only.
Does this self-check give a diagnosis?
No — and no website can. The check-in is a gentle mirror to help you notice patterns, not a diagnosis. Only a qualified professional can assess your mental health. Whatever your score, support is available.
I'm worried about cost and distance.
The national helpline 1166 is completely free from all mobile networks, 24/7. TPO Nepal's line is free from Nepal Telecom (9am–5pm). This prototype's "booking" is simulated and stores nothing outside your device — it demonstrates how a future municipal tele-counselling service could work.
Why does this page exist on an urban planning site?
The Baglung IUDP treats wellbeing as part of the plan: health infrastructure, public spaces, and social services are all urban development. This portal is an academic prototype exploring how the municipality could extend mental-health access online, especially for wards far from Baglung bazaar. It was designed following WHO safe-messaging guidance.
